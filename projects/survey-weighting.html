Executive summary.

The survey study compares naive, design-weighted, calibrated, and trimmed estimates against known synthetic population truth. Calibration improves the naive estimate under within-stratum random response, while an outcome-dependent nonresponse scenario remains biased.

Question and data

How much can known population controls correct unequal sampling and nonresponse—and where does weighting fail?

A finite population of 50,000 synthetic people is divided into six poststrata. A disproportionate without-replacement sample contains 1,150 people; 715 respond in the primary scenario. Response probabilities differ strongly by poststratum. A separate failure scenario additionally makes positive outcomes less likely to be observed.

Pipeline and evaluation

Base weights are population count divided by sample count. Respondent weights calibrate each poststratum exactly to its known population total. A stratified finite-population variance and approximate t interval condition on respondent counts; a 600-repeat within-stratum bootstrap provides another diagnostic. A 95th-percentile weight cap followed by global rescaling is a trimming sensitivity analysis, not recalibration to every poststratum.

Known population prevalence is 0.5926. The naive respondent estimate is 0.4182, the base-weighted estimate 0.5133, and the calibrated estimate 0.5530, with approximate 95% interval [0.4981, 0.6079]. Kish effective sample size is 289.9 despite 715 respondents. Under outcome-dependent response, the calibrated estimate falls to 0.3905: correct population totals alone do not repair missingness within poststrata.

Assumptions and limitations

The primary estimator assumes respondents are a random sample within each poststratum and the population controls are correct. Empty or sparse response cells undermine calibration and uncertainty. Kish effective sample size describes weight concentration rather than a complete complex-survey design effect. The example has no clustering, multistage sampling, uncertain controls, or real respondents.

Reproducible delivery

Importable Python modules separate configuration, data validation, domain-specific transformations, modeling or estimation, evaluation, artifact persistence, and reporting. The command-line entry point reproduces the full study with documented dependencies and seed 42.

The private repository includes mathematical and data-contract tests, a data fingerprint, configuration and library versions, machine-readable metrics, result tables, and the evaluation report. CI runs Python 3.11 and 3.12 and checks that a fresh run reproduces the saved outputs.

Code access. Source code, the full analytical design, and detailed verification logs are available on request. The project repository is private.
