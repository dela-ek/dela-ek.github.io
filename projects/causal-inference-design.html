Decision and analysis specification

Question: does guided onboarding increase seven-day activation? The simulation fixes the endpoint, seed, sample sizes, and estimators before examining outputs. This is an explicit simulation specification, not a real preregistered field experiment. A real launch decision also needs implementation costs, retention and adverse-outcome guardrails; this study supplies none of those.

The primary randomized estimand is E[Y(1)−Y(0)], expressed as an absolute risk difference. Use every assigned user, regardless of actual exposure: intention-to-treat. Complete follow-up, correct assignment logging, independent assignment, consistent versions of treatment, and no interference are assumed. There is no optional stopping, subgroup selection, or multiple primary endpoints. A/B uncertainty uses an unadjusted Welch large-sample interval on binary outcomes. At the default sample size this is a normal approximation; it is unsuitable for tiny samples or extremely rare events.

The power calculation assumes 24% baseline activation, equal allocation, independent users, 80% power, a two-sided 5% test and a small-effect normal approximation. It is a planning MDE, not retrospective power.

Observational design

Market adoption is selected on baseline engagement, making the post-only comparison confounded. For each market i, calculate Δ_i = mean(Y_i,post) − mean(Y_i,pre). The estimate is mean(Δ_i | adopting) − mean(Δ_i | comparison). Every market has equal weight; this is not a user-weighted effect. In this complete balanced panel it equals the treatment-by-post coefficient in an OLS model with market and calendar-month fixed effects.

Each market contributes one change score. Welch's standard error compares the market changes in the two cohorts and allows their variances to differ. This handles arbitrary within-market dependence through aggregation, conditional on independence across markets. It does not treat the 1,920 rows as independent observations. Inference is approximate with 80 markets per group; correlated regional shocks or few clusters would require another uncertainty procedure.

Identification requires:

  1. Parallel mean untreated trends for adopters and comparison markets over the target pre/post windows.
  2. No anticipation before month zero and no spillovers into comparison markets.
  3. Consistent market membership, measurement, and treatment; no differential attrition.
  4. No adopter-specific concurrent shock that changes outcomes at rollout.

These assumptions are encoded in the clean simulation. The violation scenario changes only the adopter-specific untreated slope; its true effect remains +4 pp. Neither adding fixed effects nor a precise interval repairs this violation. Common adoption avoids the comparison problems of staggered treatment timing; do not reuse this estimator as a general staggered-adoption solution.

Diagnostics

  • Sample-ratio mismatch: Pearson chi-square versus 50/50 assignment. This can flag logging or assignment problems, but a nonsignificant result does not prove correct randomization.
  • Balance: baseline engagement standardized mean difference. This is a descriptive diagnostic, not an adjustment or a selection rule.
  • Event study: compare each cohort's within-market change from month −1 for every month. Pointwise Welch intervals do not control familywise coverage. The reference coefficient is fixed to zero, not estimated.
  • Joint leads: a Wald chi-square statistic uses the full covariance matrix of five pre-period differences. It is asymptotic and does not establish the unobserved post-treatment counterfactual.
  • Placebo: set a fake rollout at month −3 using only the six genuinely untreated months. It checks for an earlier spurious break and cannot rule out a later concurrent shock.
  • Sensitivity: add +0.6 pp/month to untreated adopter trends. The post and pre time averages differ by six months, creating +3.6 pp bias in the DiD estimate. The event-study visualization shows the violation.

Scope

No real customer records, actual intervention, or measured business returns are used. The study does not model noncompliance, missing outcomes, treatment heterogeneity, staggered adoption, interference, or post-selection inference. Extend the data-generating process and identification strategy together before applying the code to such settings. The randomized and observational samples are separate hypothetical studies: their point estimates should not be pooled as independent evidence about a real intervention.
