Executive summary.

Which customers benefit from a retention campaign, rather than simply having high churn risk? The study selects an S-learner on validation policy value, then evaluates it on 3,600 held-out customers. The estimated incremental value is 4.40 synthetic units per eligible customer, with pointwise approximate 95% interval [1.16, 7.64].

Question and data

The simulator creates 18,000 independent customers, randomized 50/50 to a retention contact. Low-engagement customers can benefit, while some highly engaged customers are harmed. Measured engagement, tenure, and support history are pretreatment predictors.

Customers are split 60/20/20 into training, validation, and test cohorts. A 30% capacity rule targets exactly 1,080 test customers. Contact costs 2 synthetic units; retention is valued at 100 units. These are illustrative constants.

Pipeline and evaluation

A T-learner fits separate conditional-mean forests for treatment and control. An S-learner fits one forest including assignment. Only validation outcomes determine model selection; hidden simulator probabilities and treatment effects never enter training or selection.

Held-out inverse-propensity weighting estimates policy gain versus no contact. Equal-budget alternatives target predicted untreated churn risk or random customers. The saved model supports scoring and capacity allocation on a new cohort.

Interpretation and limitations

At equal capacity, simulator oracle value is 3.789 for uplift targeting, 3.365 for risk targeting, and 1.280 for random targeting. Oracle values use known synthetic effects solely as a diagnostic; real campaigns would not reveal these quantities.

The estimated policy intervals are pointwise approximations and do not establish statistically significant superiority over other policies. Randomization, overlap, full follow-up, and no interference support identification; observational campaigns require additional methods.

Reproducible delivery

Importable Python modules separate configuration, data validation, features, fitting, prediction, evaluation, artifact persistence, and orchestration. A thin command-line entry point matches the existing project conventions.

The repository includes a documented data contract, saved-model inference, mathematical and leakage checks, reference predictions, machine-readable metrics, and a manifest recording versions, configuration, and data fingerprints. CI tests Python 3.11 and 3.12 and reproduces held-out outputs.
