Case study 05 · Causal inference

Does guided onboarding increase activation?

A randomized A/B experiment and observational rollout study that show how research design changes the answer—even when the true effect is known.

Executive summary.

The known synthetic effect is +4 percentage points. The randomized experiment estimates +4.37 pp and difference-in-differences estimates +3.57 pp. A post-only comparison estimates +8.09 pp because adopting markets started stronger; breaking parallel trends pushes DiD to +7.17 pp.

Question and study design

Does guided onboarding improve seven-day activation, or do the markets that adopt it simply have higher baseline engagement? The project uses two separate hypothetical studies to distinguish these explanations.

  • Randomized experiment: 12,000 users independently assigned 50/50 to guided or standard onboarding. The estimand is the intention-to-treat risk difference.
  • Observational rollout: 160 markets observed across six pre- and six post-adoption months. The 80 markets with the highest baseline engagement adopt at month zero. The estimand is the equally weighted average effect on adopting markets.

Results in context

+4.37 ppA/B · 95% CI [2.79, 5.95]
+3.57 ppDiD · 95% CI [3.16, 3.99]

The valid-design DiD interval narrowly misses the known truth in this sample. A 95% interval describes repeated-sampling coverage; it does not guarantee coverage in every run. The fixed seed is retained, and the limitation is reported.

Assumptions and diagnostics

The A/B analysis checks allocation balance and baseline engagement, with all assigned users retained. Its planned minimum detectable effect is 2.18 pp at 80% power under a 24% baseline activation rate and a two-sided 5% test.

DiD compares each market’s post-minus-pre mean, then compares these changes across cohorts. Uncertainty uses independent markets, preserving dependence within each market. Causal interpretation requires parallel untreated trends, no anticipation or spillovers, stable measurement, and no adopter-specific concurrent shock.

  • Joint pre-period Wald test: p = 0.314 in the clean simulation. Passing this test does not prove parallel counterfactual trends.
  • Fake rollout in the pre-period: −0.07 pp, with 95% CI [−0.50, 0.35].
  • A +0.6 pp monthly differential untreated trend adds exactly +3.6 pp of bias to the DiD estimate.

Reproducible delivery

The public repository includes transparent data generators, estimators, twelve tests, three visualizations, data fingerprints, and a one-command report pipeline. GitHub Actions tests Python 3.11 and 3.12 and checks numerical and report reproducibility.

Read the design and assumptions · Inspect CI runs

Scope. These are simulation findings, not measured business outcomes. Common adoption timing and constant effects are assumed; staggered adoption, missing outcomes, noncompliance, interference, and treatment heterogeneity require extensions to the design.
