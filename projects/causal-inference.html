Executive summary.

Randomized email conversion and paired-store employment DiD, with balance, attrition and identification limits. randomized email customers: 42,613; paired policy-study stores: 378.

Question and real data

Did a randomized email affect conversion? How did employment change across NJ and PA after the 1992 minimum-wage increase?

  • Kevin Hillstrom email marketing randomized experiment (2008)

    Original 64,000 rows; both pipelines preselect Mens E-Mail versus No E-Mail. Womens E-Mail is excluded before analysis. Two-week conversion outcome; no retention or lifetime-value labels are invented.

  • Card and Krueger New Jersey/Pennsylvania fast-food study (1992)

    Original SHEET 407 is reused across two distinct state/chain records; immutable row ID preserves all 410 rows. Fixed-width parsing per original codebook; FTE=full-time + managers + 0.5 part-time. Paired complete observed interviews only; closed/nonresponding stores excluded, with counts reported.

Pipeline and evaluation

The email analysis preselects Mens E-Mail versus No E-Mail and reports intention-to-treat conversion risk differences with independent-unit Welch intervals, sample-ratio checks and preassignment balance. The policy analysis computes paired store FTE changes, then the NJ-minus-PA difference with store-level Welch uncertainty. Reusing each store before and after preserves pairing. Neither analysis uses hidden potential outcomes.

Assumptions and limitations

The email experiment requires random assignment, no interference and consistent outcome measurement. The observational DiD requires parallel counterfactual trends and no state-specific coincident shocks; only two survey waves exist, so pretrends cannot be tested. Independent-store intervals do not account for state-level common shocks (only two states); they must not be read as robust policy-level inference. Complete interviews exclude closures/nonresponse; attrition may bias the policy estimate. These historical studies do not establish present-day business or clinical effects.

Reproducible delivery

The Python package separates verified data loading, domain transformations and estimators, analysis orchestration, result tables, scientific figures and reporting. Offline reproduction uses immutable real inputs; source acquisition and preparation scripts separately verify checksums. Current reference outputs contain actual observations and estimates.

Tests check source contracts, mathematical behavior and evaluation boundaries. The repository workflow tests Python 3.11 and 3.12, reruns the analysis and compares input fingerprints, metrics, tables and reports. Detailed source and CI logs are available on request.
